# Adapt the idea, not just the city label

## Intake and scene design

Write a one-sentence journey, then a short table with time, subject/action, paper ground, scene fragments, crop/scale, trajectory, text and transition. Preserve explicit language, typography and output constraints. Use the user's prior approved assets and rejected decisions.

| Request | Useful design choice |
|---|---|
| 5 seconds | One built-up scene with at least two short action cycles and a readable end; avoid three title cards |
| 8–10 seconds | Two or three compact viewing scales; one sustained action interval; one or two purposeful image wipes |
| 15–30 seconds | Several daily-life beats with varied scene density and a collected ending |
| Longer story | Establish an actual journey; spend time on actions rather than repeating the same template |
| Same subject, new city | Change scene vocabulary, composition, view scale, entry timing and path; do not merely swap landmarks |
| Product | Lock packaging and text; rotate whole cutout, open/close packaging or use usage-stage variants if appropriate |
| Food/café | Ingredients → preparation objects → serving; cup/sleeve/receipt can lead a transition |
| Travel | Transit → street → destination; maps/tickets help if accurate and legible |
| Animal/object instead of person | Use anatomically appropriate pose states or mechanical states, not human gait rules |
| Multiple people/outfits | Give each a stable ID and pose set; use group layout only where readable |
| Landscape/square | Recompose visual hierarchy and paths; do not just crop a vertical export |
| Only upper-body source | Prefer portrait/café/action detail; if full body is needed, disclose that unseen clothing/body must be generated |
| Exact garment/logo requirements | Preserve from reference or composite supplied asset; review text/closures before motion |
| English-only copy | Keep titles in English; do not add a Chinese translation by habit |
| Chinese/other scripts | Use a licensed font covering all glyphs; editable text, then paper-card treatment |
| Single-paper transition | Use one opaque relevant cutout with a verified cover interval; additional decorative fragments must not masquerade as a single wipe |
| No audio | Export silent; do not add music automatically |

## Differentiation ledger

For repeated films, record previous vs proposed values for: shot order, full/half/detail ratio, subject orientation, world-motion direction, foreground trajectory, wipe object/direction, paper palette, scene vocabulary, ending. Change several dimensions that serve the story; do not change identity just to make a variant different.

Actual examples: Shanghai used four poses, street/café layers and several large object directions. London added full/half/detail cutouts, then one-image wipes and more streetside objects. Paris opened on outfit details, walked diagonally through a blue paper street and shrank a giant postcard into the ending. Those are examples, not mandatory templates.

## Scene assets have jobs

Choose enough to describe activity, rather than a fixed number: establishing architecture, ground/contact cue, street furniture/transport, lifestyle object, one transition carrier. A building alone plus a city label usually reads as a poster. Bookstalls, café tables, plants or a passing taxi give the protagonist a place to be. Layer entry should be staggered and motivated, with quiet reading time after the build.

## Short copy

Use a primary title, a few useful location/activity notes and an optional ending. Newspaper-cut letters may vary paper colour, typeface, angle and size, but do not destroy reading order. Generated background newspaper text is texture; meaningful text should be real editable text. Do not invent editorial claims, addresses or historical facts.
